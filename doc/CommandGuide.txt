How to read this guide
- All commands are slash commands typed in chat.
- Command names are case-insensitive.
- Use <required> for required parameters and [optional] for optional ones.
- If a command takes several positional arguments, quote names with spaces when needed.
- Common target shortcuts:
  me = your active character
  here = your current structure or solar system, depending on the command

TRAVEL, MOVEMENT, FX, AND SYSTEM LOADING
----------------------------------------

/dock
What it does: Jumps you to your home/clone station.
Parameters: none.
Example: /dock

/solar <system name>
What it does: Teleports your session to another solar system and updates session identity/local membership.
Parameters:
- <system name> = solar system name
Example: /solar Jita

/tr <me|characterID|entityID> <destination|bookmark=bookmarkID|pos=x,y,z|offset=x,y,z>
What it does: Transports a character session or runtime entity.
Parameters:
- <me|characterID|entityID> = who or what to move
- <destination> = a solar system, station, static/live space entity, NPC agent, or accessible bookmark ID
- bookmark=bookmarkID = explicitly select an accessible personal or shared bookmark
- pos=x,y,z = absolute coordinates
- offset=x,y,z = offset from the current/target context
Examples:
- /tr me Jita
- /tr me 3010959
- /tr me bookmark=900001708
- /tr me pos=0,0,0
- /tr me offset=0,0,5000
- /tr 90000001 offset=0,0,10000
Notes:
- Session targets can cross stations/systems.
- Station-backed NPC agents resolve to their station; in-space agents require a materialized spatial target.
- A bare bookmark ID is also accepted after existing destination ID types have been checked.
- Runtime entities are point-move only and must stay in their current solar system.

/effect <name>
/effect stop
/effect stop <name>
What it does: Plays or stops self FX on your ship.
Parameters:
- <name> = effect key from the playable effect registry
Examples:
- /effect microjump

- /effect stop microjump
Notes:
- /effect with no argument, /effect list, /effect help, and /effect ? show the available effect list.
- You must be in space.

ITEM, SHIP, AND FIT SEEDING
---------------------------

/giveitem <item name|typeID> [amount]
/item <item name|typeID> [amount]
What they do: Add an item stack or singleton item to your station hangar when docked, or your active ship's cargo hold in space.
Parameters:
- <item name|typeID> = exact item name or typeID
- [amount] = stack quantity, default 1
Examples:
- /giveitem Tritanium 10000
- /item "Dual Heavy Beam Laser II" 8
Notes:
- In space, the items must fit in your cargo hold. Capsules have no cargo hold.
- /item is an alias of /giveitem, not an item inspector.

/fit me <module name|typeID>
What it does: Fits one matching module from your active ship's cargo into the first free compatible slot. Creates a new module only when none is available in cargo.
Parameters:
- <module name|typeID> = module name or typeID; names may be quoted
- me = your currently boarded ship; the client may send this as "me"
Examples:
- /fit me 3636
- /fit me "Damage Control I"
- /fit me Rocket Launcher I
Notes:
- Works docked or in space. Existing fitted modules are preserved.
- Searches your active ship's cargo first, not your station hangar or other containers.
- Takes one module from a packaged stack. Reusing an assembled module preserves its identity, metadata, and reactivation cooldown.
- Repeat the command to fit multiple copies; each uses the next free compatible slot.
- Feedback states whether a module was fitted from cargo or newly created.
- Checks skills, hull restrictions, hardpoints, rig limits, and available slots.
- Brings the module online if fitting resources allow; otherwise fits it offline and reports why.
- Rejected fits do not create an item. Charges and hulls cannot be fitted with this command.

/unfit me <module name|typeID|itemID|all>
What it does: Offlines and removes one matching module, or strips every fitted item with "all".
Parameters:
- me = your currently boarded ship; quoted "me" is also accepted
- <module name|typeID> = remove one matching fitted module; names may be quoted
- <itemID> = remove a specific fitted module by its inventory item ID, not its typeID
- all = remove every fitted item, including rigs, subsystems, and loaded charges
Examples:
- /unfit me Rocket Launcher I
- /unfit me 10629
- /unfit me "Damage Control I"
- /unfit me all
Notes:
- Moves the module and loaded charges to your hangar when docked, or cargo in space.
- Stops active module effects and offlines modules before moving them. Items are preserved, not deleted.
- Removes the first matching slot; use the module itemID to choose a specific copy.
- Checks cargo capacity before offlining, including capacity lost when cargo-expanding modules are removed. Insufficient space leaves the fit unchanged; free space or dock and retry.
- Individual rigs and subsystems require their normal fitting controls.
- "all" strips every fitting slot, including rigs, subsystems, and loaded charges, preserving the items. This testing override leaves existing cargo and drones untouched.
- After unfitting in space, /fit me can reuse the removed modules from cargo.

SPACE OBJECTS, DEBRIS, DUMMIES, AND DEATH TESTS
-----------------------------------------------

/celestial <typeID>
What it does: Spawns any published or unpublished type in the Celestial category (category 2), including all of its groups, in front of your ship.
Parameters:
- <typeID> = exact type ID whose categoryID is 2
Examples:
- /celestial 2016
- /celestial 30642
Notes:
- You must be in space.
- The object is rigid but not massive, so ships do not bump into it.
- Its edge is placed 1 km beyond your ship's edge.
- The object is a visual, runtime-only spawn; category-specific services such as gate travel or wormhole transit are not created.
- The object is not persisted and clears on server restart.

/lco <typeID>
What it does: Spawns a runtime-only Large Collidable Object (group 226) in front of your ship.
Parameters:
- <typeID> = exact Large Collidable Object type ID
Example: /lco 72270
Notes:
- You must be in space.
- The object is rigid but not massive, so ships do not bump into it.
- Its edge is placed 1 km beyond your ship's edge.
- The object is not persisted and clears on server restart.

SOVEREIGNTY COMMAND FAMILY
--------------------------

The server follows today's retail sovereignty: the Sovereignty Hub is the only sovereignty structure, and a claimed Hub is the claim. There are no Territorial Claim Units. Every command below works on conquerable nullsec systems with no faction owner; empire systems such as Jita are refused.

/sov help
What it does: Shows sovereignty help.
Example: /sov help

/sov status [here|systemID]
What it does: Shows sovereignty state for a system: owner, claim, indices, the Hub's engine state (window, score, reinforcement exit) and the command nodes of a running event.
Examples:
- /sov status
- /sov status 30004759

/sov anchor [here|systemID] [allianceID] [corporationID]
What it does: Places a claimed Sovereignty Hub 100 km above the sun's warp-in point and makes it the system's claim. Without IDs the caller's alliance and corporation own it. A Hub already in the system is reused and keeps its position.
Examples:
- /sov anchor
- /sov anchor 30004759 99012345 98012345

/sov anchor neutral [here|systemID]
What it does: Places an SCC-owned Sovereignty Hub at the sun with no claim, the state a player launch produces. An alliance claims it with an Entosis Link.
Examples:
- /sov anchor neutral
- /sov anchor neutral 30004759

/sov deploy <tenebrex|pharolux|ansiblex|all> [here|systemID] [allianceID] [corporationID]
What it does: Deploys sovereignty flex structures.
Examples:
- /sov deploy all
- /sov deploy ansiblex 30004759 99012345 98012345

/sov prime <hour> [allianceID]
What it does: Sets alliance prime time hour.
Examples:
- /sov prime 20
- /sov prime 18 99012345

/sov capital <here|systemID|clear> [allianceID]
What it does: Sets or clears alliance capital system state.
Examples:
- /sov capital here
- /sov capital 30004759 99012345
- /sov capital clear 99012345

/sov index <strategic|claimed|military|industrial> <value> [here|systemID]
What it does: Sets a development index level, or the claim age in days.
Parameters:
- strategic = a whole level from 0 to 5 (0, 7, 21, 35, 65, or 100 claimed days)
- military or industrial = a whole level from 0 to 5; values above 5 remain raw points for existing scripts (9,600,000 points per level)
- claimed = whole claimed days, as with /sov age
Examples (set all three indices to level 5):
- /sov index military 5
- /sov index industrial 5
- /sov index strategic 5
Other examples:
- /sov index strategic 3 30004759
- /sov index claimed 21
- /sov index military 9600000
Note: strategic and claimed require an existing alliance claim and update both its timestamp and strategic index. Wait 15 seconds, then close and reopen the Sov Hub window to refresh its cached claim age.

/sov age <days> [here|systemID]
What it does: Sets how long an alliance claim has been held, and the strategic index days that go with it.
Parameters:
- <days> = whole days counted back from now; 0 resets the claim to now
Examples:
- /sov age 21
- /sov age 35 30000798
- /sov age 0 here
Note: The system must already have an alliance claim. Repeating the command sets the age again rather than adding days. Cynosural Navigation needs 21 days and Cynosural Suppression needs 35. Wait 15 seconds, then close and reopen the Sov Hub window to see the new claim date.

/sov vuln <seconds|clear> [here|systemID]
What it does: Opens the claimed Hub's vulnerability window for the given seconds, starting now, in the capture engine that the Entosis tick reads. clear forgets the GM window so the daily window applies again. clear is refused while the Hub is reinforced or in a command node event.
Examples:
- /sov vuln 3600
- /sov vuln clear 30004759

/sov reinforce [exitSeconds|now] [here|systemID]
What it does: Reinforces the claimed Hub as if attackers had run its score down. Without an exit the engine picks its own time, about 48 hours out. exitSeconds sets the exit that many seconds from now, and now starts the command node event at once with 4 nodes in the constellation.
Examples:
- /sov reinforce
- /sov reinforce now
- /sov reinforce 600 30004759

/sov node <nodeID|next> <defender|attacker|allianceID> [here|systemID]
What it does: Captures one command node of the running event for a side. next takes the first live node. defender is the owning alliance; attacker is the caller's alliance when it is not the defender; an alliance ID names the attacker directly. The score starts at 60% for the defender and moves 7% per node, so 6 defender captures secure the Hub and 9 attacker captures destroy it.
Examples:
- /sov node next defender
- /sov node 1140000000123 99012346
- /sov node next attacker 30004759

/sov ff <seconds> [here|systemID]
What it does: Fast-forwards sovereignty timers: the Hub's engine window and reinforcement exit, the displayed timestamps, and the alliance prime hour and capital timers.
Examples:
- /sov ff 3600
- /sov ff 86400 30004759

/sov capture <allianceID> <corporationID> [here|systemID]
What it does: Claims the system for the given alliance/corporation with an owned Sovereignty Hub at the sun. Only conquerable nullsec systems with no faction owner are accepted; empire systems such as Jita are refused.
Examples:
- /sov capture 99012345 98012345
- /sov capture 99012345 98012345 30004759

/sov lose [allianceID|0] [corporationID|0] [here|systemID]
What it does: Forces sovereignty loss. With an alliance and corporation the system is transferred to them; otherwise the claim, the Hub and its command nodes are removed.
Examples:
- /sov lose
- /sov lose 0 0 30004759
- /sov lose 99012345 98012345 30004759

/sov clear [here|systemID]
What it does: Clears sovereignty state for a system.
Examples:
- /sov clear
- /sov clear 30004759

ADDITIONAL COMMAND REFERENCE
============================

These entries extend the command families above. Examples containing <IDs> are
templates: substitute IDs from your server.
The server also accepts a dot prefix, such as .container1.

LCO editing notes
- /lco <typeID> above spawns a runtime-only object. The editing subcommands below
  operate on eligible authored props, not on those newly spawned objects.
- Use /lco list to obtain the runtime item ID. Editing requires staff/admin access
  and a public solar-system scene.
- move and offset use metres; rotate takes degrees about X, Y, Z. inspect reports
  the stored rotation tuple as yaw/pitch/roll.
- Changes are live previews until /lco save. Saves use the authored source identity.

POS notes
- POS targets accept an item ID, exact name, or here; use IDs to avoid ambiguity.
- Force-state commands require staff/GM access. operate, bridge, compress, and
  production use their normal access checks; forcing production cycle also
  requires GM authority and supported production data.
- These diagnostics expose server operations even where legacy client menus are
  unavailable. Unsupported historical production configurations can be refused.

Abyssal diagnostics below require staff/admin access. Normal entry uses filaments.
Single-rack fitted-ship commands, /lesmis, and /miner require docking.

What it does: Restores the character's starter skill profile.
Example: /backintime me

/allskins [target]
What it does: Grants the supported SKIN licenses.
Example: /allskins me

/allskinr [target]
What it does: Unlocks every supported unlimited SKINR component and adds 1,000 uses of each supported limited component directly to the character's component library.
Parameters: [target] defaults to me; accepts a character ID or exact character name.
Example: /allskinr me
Notes: Repeating the command adds another 1,000 uses of each limited component. Already owned unlimited components are retained. The grant is saved together, and the online character receives component-grant notices.

/skinr
/skinr list [target]
What it does: Lists saved SKINR designs, their UUIDs, and design-slot usage.
Parameters: [target] defaults to me; accepts a character ID or exact character name. /skinr with no arguments is shorthand for /skinr list me.
Example: /skinr list me
Notes: UUIDs identify saved drafts. The delete and export commands accept the compact 32-character UUID or the hyphenated form printed by this command. See SKINR saved-design capacity below for skill and server-setting limits.

/skinr delete <draft UUID|all> [target]
What it does: Deletes one saved SKINR design, or all saved designs for the target, to free design slots.
Parameters: Use a UUID from /skinr list. [target] defaults to me; accepts a character ID or exact character name.
Examples:
- /skinr delete <draft UUID> me
- /skinr delete all me
Notes: This removes saved drafts only. Sequenced SKIN licenses and designs retained for sequencing jobs are preserved. Relog if the saved-design list is still cached.

/skinr export <draft UUID> [target]
What it does: Writes a portable .skinr.json file containing a saved design's hull, components, colors, pattern layout and editor settings.
Parameters: Use a UUID from /skinr list. [target] defaults to me; accepts a character ID or exact character name.
Example: /skinr export <draft UUID> me
Notes: The reply gives the full file path under _local/skinr/designs (or skinr/designs under EVEJS_DATA_ROOT). Each export creates a new file. Send the file through Discord or another file-sharing method.

/skinr import <filename.skinr.json> [target]
What it does: Reads a portable design file and creates a new saved draft for the target character.
Parameters: Place the file in the server's skinr/designs folder and pass only its filename. Quote filenames containing spaces. [target] defaults to me; accepts a character ID or exact character name.
Example: /skinr import "My Design.skinr.json" me
Notes: Import validates the file format/version, hull, component catalog, layout and editor settings, and requires a free saved-design slot. The maximum file size is 64 KiB. It preserves the source file and creates a new draft UUID. Component rights, SKIN licenses, wallets and sequencing jobs are not imported; the recipient supplies their own component licenses when sequencing. Relog if the saved-design list is still cached.

Sharing a SKINR design through Discord or another file-sharing service:
1. Save the design in the in-game SKINR editor, then run /skinr list to obtain its UUID.
2. Run /skinr export <draft UUID>. The reply gives the full path of the exported .skinr.json file.
3. Send that file to the recipient. The recipient copies it into their server's _local/skinr/designs folder (or skinr/designs under their EVEJS_DATA_ROOT), creating the folder if needed.
4. The recipient runs /skinr import "received filename.skinr.json". This creates their own saved draft, using one of their available design slots.
5. Relog if needed, then load the imported draft in SKINR. To produce a finished SKIN, sequence it using the recipient's own component licenses and normal sequencing resources.
Notes: Both installations must support the exported hull and components. Repeating an import creates another saved draft. Exporting does not remove the sender's saved draft.

SKINR saved-design capacity:
- The standard limit is five base slots, plus Design Concepting and Design Iteration bonuses: 125 slots with both skills at V.
- To grant both skills at V for the current character, use /giveskill me "Design Concepting" 5 and /giveskill me "Design Iteration" 5, then relog.
- Server setting: skinr.skinrDesignIterationMultiplier in config/gameplay.json.
- Environment override: EVEJS_SKINR_DESIGN_ITERATION_MULTIPLIER.
- The default multiplier is 1. Values from 0 through 100, including decimals, are accepted. Only Design Iteration's earned bonus is multiplied; base slots and Design Concepting are unchanged. Fractional totals round down.
- With both skills at V: 1 gives 125 slots, 2 gives 220, 5 gives 505, and 0 leaves the 30 slots from base capacity and Design Concepting.
Example section in config/gameplay.json:
  "skinr": {
    "skinrDesignIterationMultiplier": 5
  }
Restart the server and relog after editing the setting. /skinr list reports the resulting capacity. Lowering the limit retains existing drafts and permits editing/deletion; new saves and imports require space below the new limit. The client's capacity counter uses the configured limit; built-in skill descriptions retain their standard bonus text.

EXPERT SYSTEMS
--------------

/expertsystems is an alias for /expertsystem. Use /expertsystem list to obtain
valid names/type IDs. Targets accept me, a character ID, or a supported name.

/expertsystem list
What it does: Lists Expert Systems.
Example: /expertsystem list

/expertsystem inspect <type|name>
What it does: Shows a system's definition. info is an alias.
Example: /expertsystem inspect <typeID>

/expertsystem status [target]
What it does: Shows installed systems.
Example: /expertsystem status me

/expertsystem add <type|name> [target] [days=N]
What it does: Installs a system, optionally setting duration. install is an alias.
Example: /expertsystem add <typeID> me days=7

/expertsystem remove <type|name|all> [target]
What it does: Removes installed systems. uninstall is an alias.
Example: /expertsystem remove all me

/expertsystem clear [target]
What it does: Clears the target's installed systems.
Example: /expertsystem clear me

/expertsystem giveitem <type|name> [qty]
What it does: Grants consumable Expert System items.
Example: /expertsystem giveitem <typeID> 1

/expertsystem consume <itemID>
What it does: Consumes an Expert System item.
Example: /expertsystem consume <itemID>
